# 05 — Boundaries around protective stabilization

Authored simulation. All case details are fictional. Not a base/adapter generation.

**Mode:** Challenging a convenience-based proposal and responding to withdrawal of consent.

**Case:** A child cannot remain still for a nonurgent procedure. A team member proposes protective stabilization because the clinic is behind schedule. The examiner then asks about a separate, appropriately assessed urgent-care situation.

## Conversation

**Examiner:** Does the schedule justify the proposal?

**Candidate:** No. Protective stabilization is not justified by practitioner convenience or used as discipline. I would assess the need for treatment now, the child's medical and psychological circumstances, and alternatives. Less restrictive measures and patient-specific safety considerations matter.

**Examiner:** In a separate urgent situation with a valid indication, what must be addressed before using it?

**Candidate:** I would discuss the reason, technique, benefits, risks, and alternatives with the parent; obtain and document informed consent; and seek the child's assent when appropriate. A trained team must choose the least restrictive suitable approach and monitor the patient, including breathing, circulation, pain, and distress.

**Examiner:** The parent asks you to stop during the procedure. What happens next?

**Candidate:** I would promptly take the steps needed to bring the procedure to a safe conclusion and end the restraint. I would reassess the child and document the event. Severe stress also calls for ending stabilization as soon as possible, rather than finishing for convenience.

## Debrief

- The first answer rejects convenience as an indication.
- The second names consent, individual safety, and monitoring.
- The last answer honors the request while making the procedure safe to conclude.

These are authored discussion prompts, not an official board scoring rubric.
