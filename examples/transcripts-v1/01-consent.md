# 01 — Consent when the plan changes

Authored simulation. All case details are fictional. Not a base/adapter generation.

**Mode:** Strong response with a refusal probe.

**Case:** A seven-year-old is attending with a parent. The appointment was planned for a restoration. New findings suggest a different procedure may be needed. No emergency is described.

## Conversation

**Examiner:** The parent signed the original form. What do you say before changing the procedure?

**Candidate:** I would explain the new findings, the proposed change, its benefits and risks, and reasonable alternatives, including no treatment. I would check understanding, invite questions, and update consent before proceeding. I would involve the child at an appropriate developmental level.

**Examiner:** The parent says, “I signed permission for whatever you think is best.” Is that enough?

**Candidate:** I would not rely on a broad signature alone. Consent requires a procedure-specific discussion. The method of documenting an updated decision also depends on applicable state law. I would verify that the accompanying adult has authority to consent.

**Examiner:** The parent refuses the new recommendation. Give your closing response.

**Candidate:** I would explore the concern, explain the consequences of refusal and the alternatives, and document the discussion and decision. I would not interpret refusal as permission to proceed or as removal of my standard-of-care responsibilities.

## Debrief

- The first answer identifies the trigger for a new consent discussion.
- The second distinguishes discussion from a signature and names a legal detail requiring verification.
- The final answer handles refusal without coercion or an invented legal exemption.

These are authored discussion prompts, not an official board scoring rubric.
