# 02 — Revising an incomplete behavior plan

Authored simulation. All case details are fictional. Not a base/adapter generation.

**Mode:** Deliberately weak opening, examiner probe, then correction.

**Case:** A six-year-old is frightened at an elective visit and will not sit in the chair. The parent asks you to “get it over with.” You have not assessed pain, previous experiences, or the urgency of treatment.

## Conversation

**Examiner:** Give your first proposal.

**Candidate (deliberately weak opening):** I would move straight to an advanced technique so we could finish today.

**Examiner:** That skips your assessment. What information could change your proposal?

**Candidate:** My opening was premature. I need the child's medical and developmental history, pain assessment, previous dental experiences, communication needs, and treatment urgency. I would listen to the child and parent, then individualize the plan rather than choose a technique solely to finish the appointment.

**Examiner:** Assume the assessment confirms nonurgent needs. Demonstrate a better opening and explain your next decision.

**Candidate:** “What feels worrying today? Let me explain and show you one small step, then we can talk about how it feels.” I would use developmentally appropriate communication and reassess the response. Deferred care can be considered when appropriate; advanced options need their own assessment, precautions, and consent.

## Debrief

- Treat the first candidate turn as a negative example, not a recommended plan.
- Credit the correction for assessing pain and urgency before selecting techniques.
- The final turn demonstrates communication, reassessment, and alternatives; it does not promise successful treatment today.

These are authored discussion prompts, not an official board scoring rubric.
