# 03 — Asking for evidence before assigning risk

Authored simulation. All case details are fictional. Not a base/adapter generation.

**Mode:** Appropriate uncertainty with a targeted information request.

**Case:** A parent describes a five-year-old as having “no cavities,” but reports frequent sweet snacks. The child's examination, lesion history, fluoride exposure, and usual home care have not been provided.

## Conversation

**Examiner:** Assign a final risk category from that description.

**Candidate:** I would not finalize it from that description alone. I need the age-appropriate assessment of risk factors, protective factors, and disease indicators, including clinical findings. The parent's report does not substitute for those findings.

**Examiner:** What would you ask for, rather than simply say “more information”?

**Candidate:** I would clarify the frequency of sugar exposures, fluoride exposure and home care, relevant medical and social factors, previous lesions or restorations, and the examination findings. I would weigh the overall pattern with clinical judgment rather than treat one answer as a complete score.

**Examiner:** Why perform the assessment again at a later visit?

**Candidate:** Risk assessment belongs in periodic examinations as well as the initial examination. New clinical findings or changed exposures can alter the assessment and the management pathway. I would describe what changed instead of carrying forward an unsupported category.

## Debrief

- The candidate identifies the missing evidence and asks focused questions.
- The response separates risk factors, protection, and evidence of disease.
- It avoids an invented numerical risk, fixed treatment interval, or guaranteed outcome.

These are authored discussion prompts, not an official board scoring rubric.
